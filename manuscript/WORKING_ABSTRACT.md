# Working Abstract

Recent work on neural-network self-replication, model merging, conditional backdoors, parameter-based editing, and agent-harness security motivates a concrete problem statement: could a donor model's condition-dependent behavior be used by a weight-space editor to modify a distinct recipient's own parameters, so that the recipient expresses the behavior under the relevant task or context while otherwise retaining its ordinary functions?

This repository calls that hypothetical single-transfer scenario **donor-guided conditional-behavior implantation**. Conventional model merging already provides prior art for one-generation backdoor inheritance, so the general possibility of parameter-mediated behavior transfer is not claimed as new. The narrower question concerns direct donor-guided editing and its distinction from conventional merging, prompts, adapters, external memory, checkpoint substitution, and harness modifications. Whether the transferred function is a new capability, a policy/refusal change, or task routing must also be distinguished.

A one-generation implantation is a P2 question, not proof of generational contagion. The stronger P3 question asks whether the recipient can support a further transfer using information carried in its own parameters. Conditional activation, transfer portability, collateral capability loss, model-layer changes, and harness-layer actions are separate issues.

The contribution here is a problem formulation, provisional terminology, and preliminary literature synthesis intended to help specialists and appropriately equipped research teams assess the question. The repository author is not conducting experiments, does not claim the necessary infrastructure or team, and makes no commitment to produce empirical results. No tests, implementations, or experimental findings are reported. Novelty and priority remain unestablished.
